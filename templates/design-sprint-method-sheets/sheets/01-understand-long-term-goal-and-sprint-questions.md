# Long term goal and sprint questions

*Adapted from the Design Sprint method (Knapp, Zeratsky, Kowitz). Own adaptation for teaching.*

**Day:** Monday (Understand) | **Time needed:** about 1 h

The team sets the direction of its effort for the sprint.

## Objectives (Why?)

1. Give the team a guiding point.
   - It steers solutions and decisions during the week.
   - It points the team at the most important challenge.
2. Face worst-case scenarios and assumptions.
3. Get answers from customers through the test at the end.

## Instructions (How?)

### Before starting

Look at the map and note where the problems are; they give direction.

### The activity

The exercise looks at the future through two lenses: first an optimistic one (the long term goal), then a pessimistic one (the sprint questions).

1. **Long term goal (about 30 min).** A goal that guides the sprint and what follows. A good goal:
   - inspires the team,
   - is real and can be delivered within a timeframe,
   - covers explicit and implicit human needs,
   - leaves room for many solutions,
   - is not a KPI and not too broad,
   - can be remembered later.

   Build it in three steps: everyone writes their own version (brainwriting); the team pulls out keywords from all versions; the team finalises one goal together. Keep it narrow and make sure the team agrees.
2. **Sprint questions (about 30 min).** The questions the prototype must answer; they work as the success measure of the sprint.

### When finished

Put both where everyone can see them.

## Expected outcome (What?)

- A goal that is easy to remember, inspires the team and allows different solutions.
- A set of questions to answer during the sprint.

## Key ideas

1. Iterate.
2. Be specific.
3. Put KPIs into the sprint brief, not into the long term goal.
