# Map

*Adapted from the Design Sprint method (Knapp, Zeratsky, Kowitz). Own adaptation for teaching.*

**Day:** Monday (Understand) | **Time needed:** 60-90 min

A mapping exercise that shows what users need and how they behave, set into the journey they go through with the solution or the challenge.

## Objectives (Why?)

1. Understand your users.
2. Map the user journey through your service or challenge.
3. Find the points to focus on.

## Instructions (How?)

### Before starting

Gather the data about your users from your research.

### The activity

1. **Empathy map (30 min).**
   - A quick picture of what users need.
   - Gives the team a common understanding and builds empathy.
   - Puts the team's assumptions and knowledge side by side.

   The team fills in a grid from interview notes and analytics. Separate what users say from what they actually do. Describe how they feel and what they think while dealing with the problem. Add their pains and the gains they could get from your solution.

   [The original sheet shows an empty empathy-map grid here as an illustration; it is not reproduced. Draw your own on a flipchart.]
2. **Customer journey map (30 min).** A step-by-step picture of what the customer experiences while solving the challenge, using a product or service, or reaching a goal.
3. **Map to map (30 min).** Put the needs and behaviours from the empathy map onto the journey map. The places where they meet are the points of interest for the sprint.

### When finished

Keep the map visible for the following activities.

## Expected outcome (What?)

A diagram of the user's needs and problems along the journey through your service or through the challenge you want to solve.

## Key ideas

1. Balance detail: enough context, still easy to read.
2. Start somewhere and grow the map from there.
3. Do not get stuck in early discussion before the whole map is filled.
