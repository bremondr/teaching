# 5 act interview

*Adapted from the Design Sprint method (Knapp, Zeratsky, Kowitz). Own adaptation for teaching.*

**Day:** Friday (Test) | **Time needed:** 1 h per participant (one day)

A structured interview to test the prototype: one day, one prototype, five participants from the target user group.

## Objectives (Why?)

1. Answer the sprint questions.
2. Get feedback on the prototype.
3. Confirm or disprove the idea.

## Instructions (How?)

**Learn as a team**
- The team watches the interviews together, so everyone takes in the results at the same time and the conclusions are shared.
- At the end of the day the team can decide on next steps with evidence.

**Take notes as a group**
- **Draw a grid** on a big whiteboard before the first interview: five columns (one per participant) and a few rows for each part of the prototype or sprint question.
- **Mark the mood of notes** with colours or signs such as +, - and ~ (positive, negative, neutral). Everyone writes quotes, observations and their own interpretations.
- **Stay quiet** during the interviews and take a break after each one.
- **Put notes in the grid** after each interview, in the right row and column.

**Moderator tips**
- Do not ask leading questions.
- Listen and be patient.
- Do not step in too early.
- Make the tester feel comfortable.

### Before starting

- Prepare the prototype.
- Prepare the sprint questions you want answered.
- Prepare a short story that puts the user in the situation where they would use the prototype (see [Interview script](05-test-interview-script.md)).
- Prepare a mission for the user.
- Prepare your questions.
- Do a test run of the mission.

### The activity

Run these five parts with each of the five users.

1. **Welcome.** Be friendly and make small talk so people feel free to be honest and critical. Explain how the session works and say that you are testing the product, not them.
2. **Context questions.** Start slowly with questions about the person's life, interests and activities. This builds rapport and helps you read their reactions later.
3. **Introduce the prototype.** Ask if they are willing to look at a prototype. Say that some things may not work yet. Ask them to think aloud while they use it.
4. **Tasks and nudges (the mission).** Realistic tasks are the best way to see real-world behaviour. Ask gentle questions that keep them thinking aloud.
5. **Quick debrief.** Close with questions such as how they would describe the product to a friend, or how it compares to what they do now. After each interview the team briefly shares thoughts and observations.

### When finished

Debrief after all interviews:
- Spend 5 to 10 minutes alone looking for patterns in the notes on the whiteboard.
- Each person reads their patterns aloud; the facilitator writes them down and marks them positive, negative or neutral.
- Review the sprint questions to see which patterns matter most and what could come next.
- Hold a short discussion; the decider decides on the follow-up.

## Expected outcome (What?)

1. Feedback on the prototype from real users.
2. Observations on how they use it.
3. Answers to the sprint questions.
4. A clear basis for the next steps.

## Key ideas

1. Listen.
2. Do not lead.
3. Do not judge.
4. Do not show hurt feelings.
5. Make sure testers feel well throughout.
