# 4 step sketch

*Adapted from the Design Sprint method (Knapp, Zeratsky, Kowitz). Own adaptation for teaching.*

**Day:** Tuesday (Ideate) | **Time needed:** 2-3 h

A structured way for each person to turn ideas into a well-formed concept: review the key information, start drawing on paper, try several variations, then draw one detailed solution.

## Objectives (Why?)

1. Give structure to ideation.
2. Get ideas from everyone.
3. Put more thought into each concept.

## Instructions (How?)

All steps are individual. Team members do not share or discuss intermediate results.

### Before starting

- Revisit the long term goal.
- Remind the team about the sprint questions.
- Remind yourself about the selected HMWs.

### The activity

1. **Notes (20 min).** Walk silently around the materials in the room and write down prompts for your ideas. Re-read the lightning talk notes (rose, thorn, bud) and look again at the map and customer insights.
2. **Ideas (20 min).** Alone, sketch rough ideas for solutions based on your notes. Circle the most promising ones.
3. **Crazy 8s (8 min).** The time limit stops overthinking and judging. Let the first idea go and make new variations. Fold a sheet of paper in half three times to get 8 frames and sketch a variation of your chosen idea in each, one minute per frame. An optional extra round, "Devil's 8s", is also 8 minutes (the original sheet gives no detail on it).
4. **Solution sketch (30-90 min).** Explain your concept so the team can evaluate it. Draw a three-panel storyboard with markers and sticky notes. Make it self-explanatory and specific, keep it anonymous. Ugly is fine.

### When finished

- Keep the sketches anonymous and give each a catchy title.
- The facilitator collects them and prepares the gallery for the next step.

## Expected outcome (What?)

A gallery of three-panel storyboards on A4 sheets, one per team member.

## Key ideas

1. Build on what exists: improve and mix existing ideas.
2. Anyone can sketch; boxes and words are enough.
3. Concrete is easier to judge than abstract.
4. Work alone, side by side: individual time gives better solutions than group brainstorming.
